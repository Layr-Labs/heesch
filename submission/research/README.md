## A finite 20-square outline from a near-periodic packing and a complete first halo

The submitted polyomino has exactly two hole-free coronas and exactly two hole-permitting coronas. Its two-ring witness contains 17 copies. Four surround the center in the first ring, and twelve complete the second. A 35-copy partial third ring covers 221 of the 235 required halo cells. Fourteen required sites are vacant and fourteen other empty sites lie in enclosed pockets. The hole-free defect is 28 and the hole-permitting defect is 14. The benchmark formula gives `2 + (235 − 28)/235 = 677/235`, approximately 2.880851. This is a new finite construction in our searched coordinate collection. The previously established higher-scoring benchmark shape remains ahead of it.

### Construction

We took the 198 entries with reported `Hc=Hh=2` from Craig S. Kaplan’s table of 19-square polyominoes with hole-permitting Heesch number at least two. The other 569 entries in the same table have a different hole-free value. We independently reconstructed a pair of solid rings for every selected finite seed. For each seed, we recorded exact integer isometries connecting any two point-touching copies in those rings when the first copy lies at the center or in its first ring. We expressed each neighbor in the first tile’s reference frame. Those records describe permitted candidate contacts through a complete solid local environment. Four one-cell translations of each observed contact were added as alternate positions. [Kaplan, *Heesch numbers of unmarked polyforms*](https://doi.org/10.55016/ojs/cdm.v17i2.72886)

The search changes the outline itself. Two candidate copies must occupy 40 separate cells in a finite square-grid quotient of order 41, 42 or 43. We enumerated period lattices in Hermite normal form and all square symmetries and translations permitted by the registration model. A relative orientation had to take at least one period vector outside the period lattice. This requirement allows different local neighborhoods for two representatives and makes a nearly full periodic domain a weaker condition than a true periodic tiling. We compared each registration with the actual 19-square seed before changing the tile. Among 6,321,430 evaluated registrations, 72,824 had at most three collisions under the implemented comparison. We retained 4,553 ranked registrations for the geometry experiment. The registration table retains one-based seed identifiers and the exact basis, relative isometry and source chart for each trial.

An added square has multiple images under the selected quotient poses. Reserving these images forces all overlapping old squares to be removed. The generator searches a two-step edge envelope, at most four newly occupied squares, and at most two extra removals beyond those forced by image collisions. The resulting outline must contain 20 squares, be edge connected and have no hole. Around that center we solve a separate unlabeled exact-cover problem. The selectable copies use contacts from the seed atlas and its mobile alternatives. Each active neighbor must cover some required point-halo cell and must stay disjoint from all other occupied sites. Each individual set-cover search has a 500-node bound. We independently distinguish a full halo made with an outer pocket from a solid first-corona witness. This construction rule can replace a neighbor with another position or orientation, and it does not require a new outline to reproduce the donor’s old second ring.

Candidate 96 came from entry 111 in the published table using registration `kaplan19_111_0`. In the donor’s original frame we removed `(0,3)` and `(3,6)` and added `(0,0)`, `(2,2)` and `(5,5)`. The quotient generators are `(42,0)` and `(27,1)`. Its two copies are the identity and `<0,-1,41,-1,0,0>`. Their 40 images occupy distinct residues, leaving `(27,0)` and `(28,0)` vacant. The normalized outline and the original-frame construction are geometrically congruent. `research/check.py` confirms that relation directly under all eight square symmetries, checks the five edits, rebuilds the quotient and verifies each vacancy. The resulting two-ring geometry and upper-bound refutation were generated afresh for this tile. The source donor’s Heesch number is used only to guide construction.

### Search and packing evidence

The search evaluated 24,146,679 quotient-compatible exchanges. Of these, 1,199,670 yielded a connected disk and 3,968 distinct candidates had an explicit complete first halo from the local atlas. The independent geometry checker found 1,614 solid first rings in those saved witnesses and recorded a pocket for the other 2,354. We deduplicated the 3,968 canonical coordinate sets against earlier geometry banks in the workspace. This identified 3,243 locally unseen outlines. Candidate 96 is among those 3,243. These comparison counts are bounded by the archived banks and do not establish global novelty.

A new unconstrained geometric search found 61 solid second-ring witnesses and 26 fourth-ring witnesses among the non-isohedral candidates. All 26 fourth-ring shapes reached a fifth solid ring. Exact fundamental domains with two, three, four or six copies prove every one of those 26 tiles the plane. Sixteen of the 26 were absent from the previous comparison banks. We treated search timeouts and exhausted heuristic contact screens as search outcomes. Periodicity required separate explicit residue coverage, and the finite conclusion for the submitted tile required the full UNSAT proof. For 36 geometries without an initial period, reruns with additional contact-pair possibilities recorded bounded results at depths three and four. The accompanying coordinate banks, per-template outcomes, witness search outcomes and period rows are compressed individually in `submission/research`.

For the submitted outline we searched 90 native placement orderings, independently checked each complete two-ring chart, and found 22 distinct occupied two-ring footprints. The partial-ring optimizer enumerates every congruent placement that meets a required third-ring cell while avoiding the inner patch. It chooses mutually disjoint partial copies and inserts enclosed-component cuts when a proposed selection creates sealed pockets. The winning footprint came from seed 40214. The full outline and exact transforms appear in `submission/best.heesch`. `research/packing.json` lists the 90 retained outcomes. The displayed 28 defects are reproducible from the 235-site halo, point-contact coverage and an edge-neighbor flood fill of the complement. The fraction is the best we verified in those search limits.
