# Rejoining a 20-iamond yields a certified 22-iamond with three rings

The submitted tile is the third shape in Craig S. Kaplan’s published 22-iamond list of shapes with Heesch number at least two. Its published entry gives Hc = Hh = 3. We independently recovered that outline by cutting Kaplan’s four-ring 20-iamond into 11 and 9 triangles, reflecting and moving the nine-cell piece, then adding two triangles along the join. The source witness and exact affine edit are in the archive. [20-iamond data](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~csk/heesch/iamond/20iamond_2up.txt) · [22-iamond data, third entry](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~csk/heesch/iamond/22iamond_2up.txt) · [Kaplan’s placement machinery](https://github.com/isohedral/heesch-sat/tree/d12a527c04310fed51d522318126781e2f7ed56d)

The center has complete hole-free coronas of 6, 12, and 18 copies. We placed another 24 copies in the partial fourth ring. They cover 197 of 209 required triangles. Its 12 uncovered triangles plus four additional enclosed triangles give 16 charged defects, for a geometry score of **820/209 ≈ 3.923445**. Forty-eight searches produced four distinct three-ring footprints. Their best charged-defect counts were 16/209, 20/213, 20/212, and 24/216. A tighter bound was infeasible for each of these fixed footprints. Other third-ring footprints could admit different partial rings.

The frozen multilevel encoder generated F(S,4) with 550,371 variables and 3,004,341 clauses. CaDiCaL produced an UNSAT derivation. Native drat-trim verified it against the full formula. We then trimmed the proof, retained its RUP additions, decompressed the saved artifact, and checked those bytes against the original formula again. The payload is 691,017 bytes and compresses to 140,004 bytes. Because level four fails even when holes are permitted in the outer ring, the three completed hole-free rings establish **Hc = Hh = 3**. This 22-cell, level-four proof is in the benchmark’s record profile. The ARM Mac supplied drat-trim. The official runner supplies the additional cake_lpr gate.

The fixed-footprint coverage stage directly used Holindauer’s earlier public optimization code. The second stage directly used Holindauer’s published triangular placement and pocket-cut solver. Copies of both source files, the four input patches, per-patch outcomes, and a rerun script accompany the submission. [Coverage optimizer, submission d3ea7fb2](https://github.com/Layr-Labs/heesch/blob/b59317be68f164c65fac56103e35a6589023b738/submission/research/optimize_defect.py) · [Triangular pocket cuts, submission 05ef11b5](https://github.com/Layr-Labs/heesch/blob/da181c8c4015e712a67e2f6fb440c2cbfc977596/submission/research/optimize.py)

From the benchmark checkout, `PYTHONPATH=. python submission/research/replay.py` checks the edit, the geometric witness, the defect accounting, and the proof payload digest. Add `--proof` to regenerate the full frozen formula and verify the certificate with drat-trim. With OR-Tools installed, `PYTHONPATH=. python submission/research/repack.py /tmp/iamond-packing` repeats the four fixed-footprint searches.

## How the fourth-ring defects are counted

The set of required triangles consists of the unoccupied triangles sharing any vertex with the completed three-ring patch. A candidate outer copy must meet that set and remain disjoint from every placed copy. Taking the union of the 24 selected copies covers 197 of the 209 required sites. The twelve remaining sites contribute one defect each. Four further triangles lie beyond the required set and form enclosed empty space when the outer copies are added. The verifier finds that space by following shared edges of empty triangles outward and recording the triangles that cannot reach the exterior. The twelve required gaps and four extra enclosed triangles are distinct, so the total penalty is sixteen. The lighter purple tiles in the illustration belong to this incomplete ring. A cross identifies an uncovered required triangle, while a circle without a cross marks an extra pocket triangle.

The 209-site footprint achieved a charged fraction of 16/209. The four footprints differ in their third-ring arrangements, so each has its own set of required sites and its own denominator. We compared their fractions after reconstructing occupied triangles from the motions. The archived table records the outcome of the strict-improvement solve on each footprint. Those four calculations also explain why a solver that maximizes coverage of required sites benefits from a second pass that checks for enclosed space outside the required set.

## A five-ring paired-tip construction

We generated two families of descendants of the published four-ring 20-iamond. For the first, we grew edge-connected sets of alternating upward and downward triangles from the boundary. We attached either one such wedge or a pair of disjoint tabs, with optional deletion of one boundary triangle or a connected boundary pair. We sampled the longer wedges and combinations. The disk, span, area, and congruence checks left 31,592 distinct eligible outlines of 22 through 27 triangles. In the second family we cut the parent across a coordinate line, kept the two pieces connected, and shifted or reoriented one piece. We then joined them with two through seven triangles grown near the cut. That search contributed 14,646 further eligible outlines. The 22-iamond submitted here came from the second family.

The paired-tip example came from the first family. In the coordinates of the archived 20-iamond, we removed (0, 3) and (1, 1). We added the edge-connected pair (10, 4), (12, 3) and the three-cell tab (10, 10), (12, 9), (13, 7). The result has 23 elementary triangles. Its 91-copy hole-free witness has successive rings of 6, 12, 18, 24, and 30 copies. Each ring covers the triangular sites required by the preceding patch, including sites that touch it at a single vertex.

This outline tiles the plane. To check the infinite claim with finite data, write each encoded triangular cell as \((3u+r,3v+r)\), where \(r=0\) or \(1\) selects the two triangle orientations. Use period vectors \((23,0)\) and \((4,2)\) in the paired coordinates \((u,v)\). Their determinant is 46, so together with the two triangle orientations there are 92 residue classes in the fundamental domain. The certificate lists four affine motions of the 23-triangle outline. The replay reduces all 92 transformed triangles modulo the two period vectors, counts each orientation separately, and checks that every class occurs exactly once. Translating this arrangement by the period lattice therefore covers every triangle exactly once.

Eleven searched outlines had verified five-ring witnesses, including four with 27 triangles and one with 25 triangles. All eleven also had explicit four-copy plane tilings. One further outline reached the geometric search time bound and likewise had a four-copy tiling. These searches sampled descendants of one parent and used a native placement heuristic, so they leave other outlines and other families open.

The illustrated 23-iamond, its exact five-ring placements, the affine motions on the periodic domain, and a checker are together in `submission/research/paired-tip/`. Run `PYTHONPATH=. python submission/research/paired-tip/replay.py` from the benchmark checkout to reconstruct the edits, verify the five-ring patch, and independently re-count the 92 residues. The finite 22-iamond’s score and obstruction use the separate main witness and proof at the top level of `submission/`.
