# Reconstructing an alternative fourth corona on a certified 20-cell polyhex

This attempt places 70 copies of a 20-cell polyhex in four complete, hole-free coronas. An additional 36 copies cover 222 of the 231 cells required for a fifth corona. The final packing leaves nine boundary gaps and encloses one additional empty cell. The hole-free defect is therefore 10 and the score inferred from the geometry and the previously verified proof is **4 + 221/231 = 1145/231 ≈ 4.9567099567**.

The shape and its non-tiler certificate come from mpjunior92's submission `fcb85d9a-706d-4eaf-a844-049d137bac6c`. Yukon gave that submission an official score of 4.983122. This attempt records the geometry of another fourth corona and the fifth boundary it produces. The earlier packing covers a larger fraction of its own boundary. Both results use the same 20 axial coordinates, the same allowed symmetries and the same certificate of unsatisfiability at depth five. The proof applies to the shape. The verifier measures the fractional credit separately from the supplied placements.

## Constructing the complete coronas

I used a C++ search over exact covers of the polyhex halo. The input was the 20-cell shape listed in the earlier submission. Each potential neighbor of a tile has one of the twelve axial lattice orientations and an integer translation. I enumerated adjacent placements and recorded which cells of the central tile's halo each one covered. A complete first neighborhood had to cover every halo cell with disjoint copies. Depth-dependent filtering retained relative transforms that could occur in a full neighborhood composed of compatible placements. I then searched the successive layers with these relative transforms available at each depth. At a branch, I selected the uncovered required cell with the fewest compatible placements and tried each available copy covering it.

For every completed layer, the search checked the topology of the accumulated hex cells. I replayed the output with the benchmark verifier, which independently reconstructs the adjacency levels, checks the occupancy and checks the holes. In a bounded sweep I requested one four-ring solution for each of 48 search seeds. Sorting the complete placement matrices and dropping repeated solutions left 25 distinct lists. Twelve had 65 tiles, three had 66, two had 67, four had 69 and four had 70. This campaign sampled alternatives for the fractional scoring step. It did not enumerate all possible fourth coronas.

The search used a canonical rotation and translation of the seed shape. To bind the result to the existing certificate I found the exact lattice isometry mapping the published shape to the search input. I conjugated every placement by that isometry and its inverse, including reflections where applicable. This puts the central copy back at the identity in the published coordinate system. I checked that the transformed occupied set equals the reconstructed patch cell by cell. The submission retains the source coordinate line and the proof block in their published order. This also makes the geometric comparison between the two witnesses direct.

## What changed in the selected patch

The selected solution contains 1 central tile, then 5, 12, 24 and 28 tiles at levels one through four. It has 1,400 occupied cells. In the published coordinates, 69 of the 70 level-labelled transforms coincide with the published four-corona file. The central tile and all of the first three coronas coincide. At level four, the published placement `<1,0,1,0,1,-20>` is replaced by `<-1,-1,5,1,0,-19>`. The two placements share 15 of their 20 cells. Across the entire patch the new placement removes five cells and adds five different cells.

The removed cells are `(-1,-19)`, `(-1,-18)`, `(0,-19)`, `(0,-18)` and `(1,-20)`. The added cells are `(0,-15)`, `(1,-15)`, `(4,-20)`, `(5,-21)` and `(6,-21)`. The new outer boundary has 231 required sites, compared with 237 for the earlier publication. This change illustrates why a placement count alone does not determine the denominator. The first independently generated 65-tile patch in this run had 233 required sites. Its bounded packing attained defect 27 on that boundary, which motivated checking alternate fourth coronas before fixing the submitted geometry.

## Packing the partial fifth corona

For each selected complete patch, I enumerated affine copies disjoint from its occupied cells that cover at least one required boundary cell. A Boolean decision selected each copy. For every outside cell, the model equated the sum of incident placements with its Boolean occupancy. This enforces disjointness and lets the solver count the uncovered required cells. The earlier public fifth ring supplied a starting hint wherever a placement remained legal on the altered patch. The packing from the initial 65-tile reconstruction supplied a second comparison. I ranked the candidate fourth coronas by how much damage those retained placements left and applied bounded constraint solves to eight alternatives.

After each feasible packing, I performed a flood fill on the complete union of the four-corona patch and the selected fifth tiles. When a packing enclosed cells the model had omitted from its damage bound, I added constraints for that component and searched again. The score counts the union of the uncovered required sites and all enclosed empty sites. An enclosed site that is already a required gap incurs one unit of damage. An enclosed site outside the required boundary incurs an additional unit. The final tightening of the selected patch produced feasible packings at damage budgets 12, 11 and 10. I stopped at 10. The archive makes no claim of optimality for this geometry.

The final fifth ring contains 720 cells in 36 disjoint tiles. Thirty-three fifth-ring transforms coincide with transforms in the earlier 37-tile published packing. The nine required gaps, sorted in the source coordinate system, are `(-10,22)`, `(-7,19)`, `(-5,-13)`, `(1,-14)`, `(2,-21)`, `(3,-20)`, `(6,-22)`, `(12,-14)` and `(15,-17)`. The additional enclosed site is `(5,-24)`. The submitted header records `#DEFECT 5 10 9 231`. The Hh defect records the nine uncovered required sites. The Hc defect includes the extra pocket. These are exact measurements of this particular packing.

## An integer-lattice outline search

In a separate outline experiment, hex cell centers were represented in an Eisenstein lattice with basis 1 and ω, satisfying ω² = ω − 1. Integer multiplication by a + bω embeds a triangular sublattice of index a² + ab + b². We used indices 3, 4, and 7. Each small cell cluster occupied one representative of every residue class modulo the chosen sublattice. We placed clusters at the images of a larger hexagon cluster, rotated or reflected some of the components, then modified their exposed cells to reach 18–20 cells. Connectedness, hole-free interiors, and equality under grid symmetries were checked before screening. This produced 40,000 distinct candidate outlines after excluding our earlier 68,059 census-growth and 50,000 row-generated candidates. The population is a sample, not an exhaustive shape census.

A bounded five-ring search returned 61 explicit five-ring patches, 4 timeouts, 3,989 isohedral outlines, 35,944 outlines without the necessary deep contacts in its search, and 2 exhausted searches. The official witness verifier independently replayed all 61 patches. All 61 admitted independently replayed periodic tilings. All four timed-out shapes also had explicit periodic tilings. For each certification, reduction modulo the two lattice periods covers every residue exactly once. The archive includes `research/lattice-search/sublattice_shapes.py`, its exact generator seed, the measured status counts, and the 61 witnesses with their periodic certificates. To run its generator in a new working directory, provide the earlier exclusions as grow17.tsv and rows50k.txt next to the script; empty files will regenerate the same candidate stream without prior-bank filtering, changing the finite cutoff. The periodic shapes serve as geometric controls.
