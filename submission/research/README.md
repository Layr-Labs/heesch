# Twelve missing fifth-ring sites on Kaplan’s finite 16-hex

Changing the fourth ring of our 16-hex halves the charged defect, from 24 of 221 required sites to 12 of 214. The new score is \(4+202/214=529/107=4.9439252336\ldots\). The ring counts are 1, 5, 11, 21 and 27, followed by 34 partial fifth-ring tiles. The new fourth footprint differs from the previous one at 62 hexagons. All 12 charged cells are uncovered sites of the required boundary. The previous packing missed 22 required sites and enclosed two additional empty cells. The outline and its checked depth-five DRAT proof come from our earlier 16-hex submission. [Earlier construction and proof](https://github.com/Layr-Labs/heesch/tree/fd2eccbed9675a08e3b85744b0a35ab8b71ea9ff/submission)

The uncovered axial coordinates are `(-16,23)`, `(-13,24)`, `(-12,23)`, `(-11,22)`, `(-11,23)`, `(6,13)`, `(11,10)`, `(20,-12)`, `(21,-13)`, `(21,-12)`, `(21,-8)` and `(22,-12)`. The comparison figure shows both packings at their actual lattice positions. Portions of the boundary opening on the left close when the fourth ring changes, while several small openings appear on the far right.

We enumerated the central tile’s complete first and second rings jointly. After each solution the solver blocked the entire occupied first footprint. Nine first footprints admit ring two. The same projection through rings two and three produced nine second footprints in total. Exactly one third footprint admits ring four, with 608 occupied hexagons and 154 required fourth-ring sites. All nineteen forward enumerations ended with `INFEASIBLE` after their saved footprints had been excluded. For every required site we aligned every cell of every oriented copy of the prototype, allowing all twelve lattice symmetries. Disjointness and boundary contact delimit this finite domain. The geometry checker flood-fills the complement after each candidate packing. [Earlier joint-packing algorithm](https://github.com/Layr-Labs/heesch/tree/5beed0f21826aa21131764321604e14c3d0b7bf7/submission/research)

On the sole third footprint, the maximum size of the required fifth boundary in a relaxation that permits fourth-ring pockets is 270. Let \(B\) be the boundary size and \(d\) the size of the union of missing required sites and enclosed empty cells. A higher score requires the integer inequality \(12B-214d\ge1\), which with \(B\le270\) implies \(d\le15\). The unrestricted joint model considered 68 supported fourth-ring placements and 9,025 candidate outer placements, and returned `INFEASIBLE`. A repeat with exact inner support and 1,888 additional incompatibility cuts returned the same result. These exhaustive CP-SAT computations establish the maximum fifth-ring fraction on this outline within the benchmark conventions. The inherited DRAT certificate separately establishes its finite Heesch number of four.

Section 3 of a previous public note reports a 5/247 fifth-ring defect on its explicitly listed Kaplan 16-hex, with a score of 4.979757. Those coordinates give our canonical digest `9530efb1068d15c80d007e0d6e3662a7e14a84acf7ac2b15505969ee9c28d792`. We additionally tested the inclusive inequality \(5B-247d\ge0\) over every fourth and fifth packing on the unique third footprint. Since \(B\le270\), any such packing has at most five charged cells. Both solver configurations returned `INFEASIBLE`. The earlier submission’s scored witness is a different outline. Its note gives this 16-hex numerical result without an independently replayed 16-hex placement list. Under the present charged-defect rules our exhaustive result excludes that reported fraction.
